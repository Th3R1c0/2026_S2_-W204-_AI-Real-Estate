A total of ten minutes is allocated to your team with five minutes for setup time if needed. You may divide up the

your product.
Make sure to upload a copy of your presentation to your Trello board under Sprint 2 Documentation before your
presentation.
1. Your project and your Scrum management processes:
• Team details and role
• Description and features of your application.
• The Scrum development process experienced by your group
a. VERY briefly describe the Sprint 0 and Sprint 1 experiences of the team
b. Place more emphasis on Sprint 2
c. Like the Sprint 1 review, you should describe things, such as the Sprint goal, the Definition of Done,
the Burndown chart, etc (If Unsure See Sprint 1 Assessment Document).

• Pending issues and features to be developed
• Issues faced, and lessons learnt
You should use PowerPoint slides to illustrate any of the above features.
2. The live demonstration of your product:
a. Show the User Stories that you forecasted and committed to for Sprint 2.
b. Demonstrate completed work and completed features of your product, including the new features finished
in Sprint 2.
c. You can include demos into your powerpoint slides as screenshots but you will need your producted loaded
and ready to run on your device so that the teaching team can try it out during question time.

can arrange a time for your tutors to see a final version during the final laboratory session. Please

necessary in the final presentation. Features that are more complex can be presented using video
snipplets in the presentation and/or sensor spoofing (i.e., simulating the GPS coordinates) in
development mode.
Marking criteria
This presentation is worth 5% for the team will be assessed on clarity, quality, and comprehensiveness.
The demonstration of your product is worth 5% for the team and will be assessed on clarity, usability and application
quality.

Part 3 - Individual Reflection and Peer Review (10%)
Write an individual, personal, reflection about your experience of being a team member during Sprints 0, 1 and 2.
Use the Word document template available in the Final Deliverables folder on AUT Canvas. Submit the document in
PDF via Turnitin.
What did you learn personally during these Sprints? Have your ideas about planning and running a development
project changed? What do you do differently now that you did not do before participating in this project? Are there
things you would do differently next time? Explain your ideas with examples from your team’s activities.
Consider using a reflective framework such as the Gibbs Reflective Framework to make your ideas flow more clearly
and your evaluation more rigorous.
The essay should be a minimum of 250 words long and may include pictures, diagrams, and/or references to help
explain your arguments more clearly. Your essay could be longer.

Marking Rubric:
Scoring Level Standard
Excellent (A- to A+) In addition to meeting requirements for the “Good” level, the submission is an exemplary
piece of work, completely error-free, is of “best-practice” quality, and demonstrates a
deep understanding of the subject area by the author(s).
• The product shows outstanding progress in features implemented since Sprint 1.
• Overall, the first release of the product at the end of sprint 2 shows outstanding
progress by the team.
• Comprehensive scrum artefacts have been produced to document all aspects of
the project from planning to sprinting and reflection on progress.
• Scrum artefacts produced are of best-practice quality and have been produced in a
timely, well-organized manner.
Good (B- to B+) In addition to meeting requirements for the “Satisfactory” level, the submission represents
a good understanding of the subject area by the author(s), is written in a structured and
understandable manner, and meets most assessment criteria.
• The product shows considerable progress in features implemented since sprint 1.
• Overall, the first release of the product at the end of sprint 2 shows sustained
progress by the team.
• Scrum artefacts have been produced to document most aspects of the software
development process.
• Most scrum artefacts produced are of good quality and have been produced in a
timely manner.
Satisfactory (C to C+) The submission shows some understanding of the subject area by the author(s), meets a
few assessment criteria, and/or the write-up lacks structure or is not easy to comprehend.
• The product shows satisfactory progress in features implemented since sprint 1.
• Overall, the first release of the product at the end of sprint 2 shows satisfactory
progress by the team.
• Scrum artefacts have been produced to document some aspects of the software
development process.
• Scrum artefacts produced are of reasonable or satisfactory quality or have been
produced at the last minute.
Unsatisfactory (C- or
below)
The submission fails to meet most assessment and quality criteria.
